\section{The Matrix Associated with a Linear Map}
\bx{
\en{
    \item 
    \item 
    Let $c$ be a number, and let $L: R^n \to R^n$ be the linear map such that $L(X) = cX$. 
    What is the matrix associated with this linear map? 
    \begin{Solution}
        $cI$, where $I$ is the unit $n \times n$ matrix. 
    \end{Solution}
    \item 
    \item 
    \item 
    In the text, we gave a description of a matrix associated with a linear map of a vector space into itself, with respect to a basis. 
    More generally, let $V, W$ be two vector spaces. 
    Let $\{ v_1, \dots, v_n \}$ be a basis of $V$, and $\{ w_1, \dots, w_m \}$ be a basis of $W$. 
    Let $L: V \to W$ be a linear map. 
    Describe how you would associate a matrix with this linear map, giving the effect on coordinate vectors. 
    \begin{Solution}
        Let $Lv_i = \sum c_{ij} w_j$. 
        Let $C = (c_{ij})$. 
        The associated matrix is $^{t\!} C$, and  theeffect of $L$ on a coordinate vector $X$ is $^{t\!} CX$. 
    \end{Solution}
    \item 
    \item 
    Let $V$ be the vector space generated by the two functions $f_1(t) = \cos t$ and $f_2(t) = \sin t$. 
    Let $D$ be the derivative. 
    What is the matrix of $D$ with respect to the basis $\{ f_1, f_2 \}$? 
    \begin{Solution}
        $\begin{pmatrix}
            0 & 1 \\
            -1& 0
        \end{pmatrix}$
    \end{Solution}
