    \item
    Show that the image of a convex set under a linear map is convex. 
    \begin{solution}
        \begin{proof}
            Let $L: V \to W$ be a linear map, and let $S$ be a convex set in $V$. 
            We denote the image of $S$ under $L$ by $L(S)$. 

            Let $L(v)$ and $L(w)$ be two points in $L(S)$ such that $v, w \in S$. 
            Let $0 \leq t \leq 1$. 
            Then 
            \begin{equation*}
                tL(v) + (1-t)L(w) = L[tv + (1-t)w]. 
            \end{equation*}
            Then $tv + (1-t)w$ lies in convex set $S$, whence $L[tv + (1-t)w]$ is a point in $L(S)$. 
            From the definition of convex set, we show that the image of a convex set under a linear map is convex as desired. 
        \end{proof}
    \end{solution}
