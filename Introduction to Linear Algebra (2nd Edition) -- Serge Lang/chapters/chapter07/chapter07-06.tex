    \item 
    If $g(v, w)$ satisfies the first two axioms of a determinants, prove that 
    \begin{equation*}
        g(v, w) = - g(w, v) 
    \end{equation*}
    for all vectors $v, w$. 
    This fact was used in the uniqueness proof. 
    [\textit{Hint}: Expand $g(v + w, v + w) = 0$.] 
    \begin{solution}
        \begin{proof}
            Let us derive the fact from the second axiom of a determinant. 
            We have 
            \begin{equation*}
                \begin{aligned}
                    0 &= g(v + w, v + w) \\
                    &= g(v, v) + g(v, w) + g(w, v) + g(w, w) \\
                    &= g(v, w) + g(w, v) \\
                \end{aligned}
            \end{equation*}
            by linearity in both variables, i.e. the first axiom of a determinant. 
            Thus we see that $g(v, w) = - g(w, v)$. 
        \end{proof} 
    \end{solution}
